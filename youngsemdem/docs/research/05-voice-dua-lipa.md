# 05 - Copy voice guide: the Service95 letter voice, adapted for youngsemdem

Research brief for the Young Seminole Democrats (youngsemdem) demo site. Prepared Monday, November 9, 2026, six days after the Nov 3, 2026 general election. Scope: how Dua Lipa writes her Service95 newsletter letters, her Book Club copy and her social captions; how the 2024-2026 political youth accounts that worked actually wrote, and what made others cringe; and a usable voice system for the site: principles, a do/don't table, vocabulary, formatting rules, an adaptation guide and 25 original sample lines.

Confidence key: **[high]** read directly in primary sources (her own published letters) or a primary data source; **[med]** reputable secondary reporting or a practitioner's own account; **[low]** single or unverified secondary source. Quotes are kept to a few words. Everything else is paraphrased. Counts marked "own count" come from my reading of the corpus listed in Appendix A, so treat them as approximate.

Internal note: this file names Dua Lipa because it is a research document. The site itself must never use her name, image, lyrics, song titles or the Service95 brand (see section 7).

---

## Headline summary

Dua Lipa's Service95 voice is a short, warm, first-person letter from a well-travelled friend who wants to hand you something good. Her weekly letters run about 150 words. They usually open on a moment from her own week (rehearsals, a painting class, a show in Taipei the night before), then preview the issue's stories. They often close with an "I hope you..." line and almost always sign off "Dua x". In 40 letters from 2022 to 2026 I counted zero emoji, sparing exclamation marks (about one per 200 words), medium-length sentences (about 22 words on average) and a lot of "incredible". Serious subjects (famine in Afghanistan, drink spiking, Uganda's anti-gay laws, Roe) get a different register. She states plain facts, says how she feels, then names one concrete thing to do, with no sparkle and no product plugs. Her social captions run on two registers: a hype mode with ALL-CAPS key words and runs of emoji, and a minimal mode for big personal moments (a date and a single heart).

The political accounts that landed with young people in 2024-2026 add three rules the site needs:

- **Plain words that translate.** Mamdani's "freeze the rent" is the model.
- **A wink, not a costume.** Kamala HQ moved fast but deliberately stayed light.
- **Show people, don't narrate the demographic.** "Gen Z is in the house!" became Politico's headline for cringe.

The recommendation is one youngsemdem voice: **the friend in your group chat who always knows the deadline and always has a good plan for Thursday night.** It is warm, curious and specific to Seminole County, with lists that come with reasons, serious moments that stay serious, and no guilt. Copy is written by bylined (demo) members, not a celebrity persona. It sits in a small set of named, recurring rituals: a weekly letter, a list of picks, one action, a spotlight and the same sign-off every time.

## 1. Findings: how Dua Lipa writes (Service95, Book Club, captions)

### 1.1 What Service95 is, and the job her letter does

- Service95 launched on Feb 3, 2022 as a free weekly newsletter covering style, arts and culture around the world. Her stated intent was to share what she had learned about cities and to connect people through shared discoveries ([Rolling Stone UK, Feb 3, 2022](https://www.rollingstone.co.uk/music/dua-lipa-launches-global-style-arts-and-culture-newsletter-service95-10782/)). **[high]**
- She told NPR it grew out of friends texting her for recommendations when they travelled. She likes planning, and the podcast feeds a curiosity that music does not ([NPR via Michigan Public, Apr 28, 2022](https://www.michiganpublic.org/2022-04-28/in-dua-lipas-ever-expanding-world-theres-no-time-limit-and-theres-no-what-ifs)). **[high]**
- The brand's self-description today: founded on the idea of being of service to a community, a "cultural concierge" of insider recommendations and global stories. The weekly newsletter carries an exclusive letter from Dua, and the Book Club promises ways to "read the world differently" ([Service95 About Us](https://www.service95.com/about-us); [Book Club](https://www.service95.com/book-club)). **[high]**
- The site's navigation shows the range: Culture, Activism, Travel, Self, Style, The Lists, Shopping, Book Club. Activism sits next to beauty and travel rather than in a separate silo ([Service95 home](https://www.service95.com/)). **[high]**
- The signup page sells community and access rather than content volume: recommendations worth your attention, community events and giveaways, plus the letter. The button reads "Sign me up!" ([Subscribe page](https://www.service95.com/subscribe-to-service95)). **[high]**

**The letter's job:** it is the friendly doorway into the issue. It gives you a reason to care, in her voice, then hands you to the stories.

### 1.2 Anatomy of a letter (structure)

From 40 letters, Jan 2022 to Jun 2026 (Appendix A), own count **[high]**:

1. **Title** in Title Case, often with a "Plus..." add-on or a trailing ellipsis: "It's Party Time! Plus 5 Of My Favourite Recent Service95 Stories" ([Dec 14, 2023](https://www.service95.com/duas-letter-join-the-party)), "Fresh starts..." ([Sep 14, 2023](https://www.service95.com/dua-lipa-fresh-starts)), "Let's Go Offline" ([Apr 11, 2024](https://www.service95.com/duas-letter-lets-go-offline)). "Let's Talk About..." recurs.
2. **Opening line** comes from her own life, or uses a "This week, we're..." frame.
   - Personal openers: rehearsing six hours a day for a tour ([Welcome, Jan 20, 2022](https://www.service95.com/welcome-to-service95)); a painting class with a drag artist ([Apr 18, 2024](https://www.service95.com/duas-letter-drag-in-all-its-glory)); performing in Taipei the night before ([Nov 21, 2024](https://www.service95.com/duas-letter-whats-on-my-gift-list)); drawing a tarot card each morning ([Jan 30, 2025](https://www.service95.com/dua-lipa-lunar-new-year)).
   - "This week" appears in 19 of 40 letters.
3. **Bridge** from her experience to the issue's theme, usually one sentence that widens "I" to "we all".
4. **Preview** of two to four stories, each named with the writer and one hook, joined by "plus", "we also have" or "and don't miss".
5. **Optional list**: "5 of my favourite..." restaurants, artists, books or spots, each with a one-line reason ([Spoken-word artists, Mar 21, 2024](https://www.service95.com/duas-letter-the-power-of-words); [Sunny Hill/Prishtina, Jul 25, 2024](https://www.service95.com/duas-letter-sunny-hill-festival-2024)).
6. **Closer**: "I hope" appears in 19 of 40 letters, with variants on finding something meaningful or loving it as much as she did. Her signature line plays on the brand name: "As always, I hope this issue serves you" ([Oct 10, 2024](https://www.service95.com/dua-lipa-letter-how-to-be-vulnerable)). Seasonal variants: "Happy gifting!", "Happy reading!" ([Jan 9, 2025](https://www.service95.com/dua-lipa-monthly-read-january-2025)).
7. **Sign-off**: "Dua x" on almost every weekly letter, and "Love, Dua x" on a special guest-edit issue ([Malala guest edit, Oct 9, 2025](https://www.service95.com/dua-lipa-malala-guest-editor)).
8. **Footer CTAs** in caps: "READ THIS ISSUE AT SERVICE95.COM", "DISCOVER THE SERVICE95 BOOK CLUB" ([Book Club launch letter, May 11, 2023](https://www.service95.com/dua-lipa-book-club)). Long essays end on a verb-led menu: WATCH, BOOKMARK, LISTEN, DISCOVER, EXPLORE, MEET ([Handmaid's Tale essay, Nov 4, 2025](https://www.service95.com/dua-lipa-on-the-handmaids-tale)).

**Recurring named series** give readers rituals to come back for **[high]**: Dua's Monthly Read, The Wish List, The Way I Work..., Checking In, The Reading List, Dua's List (2026, delivered as saved Google Maps), 10 Questions (discussion guides), Backstage With, The Guest Edit ([home](https://www.service95.com/); [10 Questions, Oct 1, 2024](https://www.service95.com/book-club-questions-lincoln-in-the-bardo); [Dua's List: Tokyo, Oct 19, 2026](https://www.service95.com/duas-list-tokyo-guide)).

### 1.3 Sentence-level traits (with counts)

Own count across the 40 letters (about 6,300 words; list items inside letters are included, so these are approximate) **[high]**:

| Trait | What I found | Example (short) |
|---|---|---|

| Person | First-person-led: I/me/my about 4.4 per 100 words, we/our/us about 1.8, you/your about 1.7. "We" is Team Service95; "you" turns up in invitations and closers. | "I'm hoping you'll join me" (Welcome) |

| Exclamation | About 0.5 per 100 words; 22 of 40 letters use at least one, usually only one. None in the serious letters (Afghanistan, spiking, Yemen). | "I can't believe it!" (Dec 19, 2024) |
| Emoji | **Zero** in all 40 letters. | n/a |
| Asides | Spaced dashes (about 1 per 100 words) and parentheses carry asides, links and gentle jokes. | "(after reading all these pieces, of course!)" (Offline) |
| Ellipsis | Used to trail into a list or leave a warm, open ending (about 0.25 per 100 words). | "a few of my favourite spots in Prishtina..." |
| Questions | Rare in weekly letters (6 in total), mostly self-answered rhetorical questions. | "Am I exhausted? Ha." (Welcome); "A good place to start?" (Afghanistan) |
| Spelling | British (favourite, cosy, organisations). | n/a |
| Humour | Light and self-deprecating, never at the reader's expense. | "Keep the seat warm for me" (Welcome) |

**The 2025-2026 drift** **[high]**:

- **The Handmaid's Tale essay** is more voice-forward ([Nov 4, 2025](https://www.service95.com/dua-lipa-on-the-handmaids-tale)): fragments for rhythm ("Pure fiction."), Gen Z idiom ("hits different"), a sharp turn ("But only for now."), and one blunt line of profanity.
- **Ask Dua**, an agony-aunt column ([Feb 12, 2026](https://www.service95.com/ask-dua-advice-column)), frames her as the friend whose judgement you borrow before a date. It promises "No platitudes" and "real talk". She thinks out loud ("Hmmm, this is a tough one"), uses IMO and mild profanity, draws on her own experience, and gives practical, non-preachy advice.
- **Dua's List** pieces (Tokyo, bookshops, date nights, record stores, 2026) are framed in the third person ("our founder"), with her own first-person blurbs underneath. The blurbs are very concrete and sensory, and every pick has a reason ([bookshops, Oct 11, 2026](https://www.service95.com/duas-list-best-independent-bookshops); [date nights, Jul 6, 2026](https://www.service95.com/duas-list-date-night-recs)).

### 1.4 How she recommends things

- **Every pick gets a reason.** In the 2026 lists, each place gets one to three sentences, with a concrete detail (what you sit at, what you order, when to book) and a personal verdict ("I go every time") ([Tokyo](https://www.service95.com/duas-list-tokyo-guide)). **[high]**
- **Numbers and lists**: "5 of my favourite restaurants in London" was in the very first letter ([Welcome](https://www.service95.com/welcome-to-service95)). Five is her default. **[high]**
- **She credits others.** Writers are named in previews; prison book-group members chose the picks in one letter ([Downview, Jun 8, 2023](https://www.service95.com/dua-lipa-at-downview-prison)); Malala took over a whole issue ([Oct 9, 2025](https://www.service95.com/dua-lipa-malala-guest-editor)). **[high]**
- **Low-pressure community.** A Book Club piece on hosting your own club passes on a host's advice: invite people who haven't finished the book, and remember "It's not an exam" ([How to host your own book club, Apr 17, 2024](https://www.service95.com/how-to-host-your-own-book-club)). The 10 Questions guides mix craft questions with "how does this make you think about your life?" questions ([Oct 1, 2024](https://www.service95.com/book-club-questions-lincoln-in-the-bardo)). **[high]**
- **The critique to learn from.** A Harvard Crimson reviewer found the jump from serious essays to light lists jarring, the lists sometimes thin on explanation, and some picks aimed at affluent readers. At worst, the mix felt tone-deaf. The reviewer suggested more deliberate planning ([Harvard Crimson, Feb 2022](https://www.thecrimson.com/article/2022/2/26/dua-lipa-newsletter-podcast-review/)). **[med]** By 2024-2026 the letters often separate serious issues from fun ones, and the lists explain every pick. youngsemdem should start there.
- **Reception.** The New Statesman (Aug 6, 2025) treats her as a credible champion of books who reaches readers that traditional book programming misses ([New Statesman](https://www.newstatesman.com/culture/books/2025/08/dua-lipa-the-peoples-critic)). **[med]**

### 1.5 How she handles serious and political subjects

There is a repeatable pattern, **[high]** from primary letters:

1. **Name it plainly, with hard facts first.**
   - Afghanistan: famine, aid and the scale of the crisis in numbers ([Feb 21, 2022](https://www.service95.com/a-note-from-dua)).
   - Pride in Kosovo: new anti-gay laws in Uganda and a record number of anti-LGBTQIA+ bills in US states ([Jun 22, 2023](https://www.service95.com/dua-lipa-kosovo-pride)).
   - Spiking: why it is still not a specific criminal offence in the UK ([Apr 3, 2023](https://www.service95.com/dua-lipa-letter-drink-spiking-in-the-uk)).
2. **Say why it's personal, briefly.** People close to her had been spiked; she is proud of Prishtina celebrating Pride; Amed Khan is her friend.
3. **Name the feeling honestly.** "Terrifying and angering"; "It isn't an easy read".
4. **Give one concrete action.**
   - Afghanistan: demand that governments loosen their grip on the Afghan economy and let aid in.
   - Spiking: stricter penalties.
   - Yemen: a list of resources and organisations to support ([Apr 25, 2024](https://www.service95.com/duas-letter-spotlight-on-yemen)).
5. **No sparkle.** These letters have no exclamation marks, no shopping and no "so excited".

**Dignity framing.** People in hard circumstances are written as agents. Introducing Nadia Murad, she stresses that Murad "is no victim" but a global champion for survivors ([Apr 7, 2022](https://www.service95.com/activists-who-inspire-me)). **[high]**

**Optimism with receipts (the Handmaid's Tale essay, Nov 2025)** **[high]**. The essay moves through four steps:

- A personal anchor: reading the book at 15 versus at 30.
- The stakes: Roe, Poland, Afghanistan, Iran.
- Counter-evidence: recent wins abroad (abortion in Mexico, same-sex marriage in Thailand, France's constitutional amendment).
- Plain imperatives: protest book bans, demand equal pay, fight for constitutional rights.

It admits a loss and refuses to treat it as final ("But only for now."), which is close to the emotional brief youngsemdem has the week after an election ([essay](https://www.service95.com/dua-lipa-on-the-handmaids-tale)).

**On social, she is more blunt and uses fewer words.** In May 2024 she shared an Artists for Ceasefire graphic on Instagram Stories with #AllEyesOnRafah, a one-line moral statement and a request for solidarity ([Billboard Canada, May 28, 2024](https://ca.billboard.com/music/music-news/dua-lipa-ceasefire-gaza-israel-hamas-war-1235694348)). **[med]** In 2022 she told NPR that social media pushes people to react instantly, leaving no room to learn and speak from the heart ([NPR via Michigan Public](https://www.michiganpublic.org/2022-04-28/in-dua-lipas-ever-expanding-world-theres-no-time-limit-and-theres-no-what-ifs)). **[high]** Lesson: when the site speaks on something serious, it does so slowly and with substance, not as a reflex.

### 1.6 Social captions: two registers

Evidence here is secondary reporting of her Instagram captions, **[med]**:

- **Hype register.** ALL-CAPS words for emphasis, multiple exclamation marks and runs of emoji.
  - Album announcement written entirely in caps ("MY 3RD STUDIO ALBUM...") ([Spin 103.8, Mar 2024](https://www.spin1038.com/music/dua-lipa-announced-her-third-album-radical-optimism-1651558); [Billboard, Mar 13, 2024](https://www.billboard.com/music/pop/dua-lipa-new-album-radical-optimism-release-date-1235632371)).
  - "4 SOLD OUT" and "New York LEGENDS" for Madison Square Garden ([Reality Tea, Sep 23, 2025](https://www.realitytea.com/?p=963621)).
  - Last tour show: triple exclamation marks, then crying-face emoji and a joke about needing tissues ([AOL, Dec 2025](https://www.aol.com/articles/dua-lipa-stuns-backless-satin-215435825.html); the link returned 404 when I checked it on Nov 9, 2026, so this rests on the search snippet: **[low-med]**).
- **Grateful register.** Sentence case, one heart emoji, thanks named to specific people, a tilde as a soft separator, and a single caps word ("MAGIC") ([HOLA!, Aug 23, 2025](https://www.hola.com/us/celebrities/20250823851767/dua-lipa-celebrates-her-30th-birthday)).
- **Milestone minimalism.** Her wedding post carried only the date and a white heart ([ABC7, Jun 2026](https://abc7news.com/post/dua-lipa-wedding-photos-grammy-winning-singer-shares-marriage-callum-turner/19221840/)). The bigger the moment, the fewer the words.

**Takeaway:** emoji and caps belong in short social and UI moments, never in the letter.

## 2. Findings: political youth voices that landed vs cringe

### 2.1 Kamala HQ (2024): speed, restraint, an "alt" voice

- **The handover.** When @BidenHQ became @KamalaHQ in July 2024, the banner adopted the lowercase, lime-green look of Charli XCX's *brat*. The bio played on the coconut-tree meme, and the account recycled opponents' mockery (Venn diagrams, "unburdened") as shared jokes ([TIME, Jul 22, 2024](https://time.com/7001188/kamala-harris-memes-campaign-brat-coconuts-unburdened-context-venn-diagrams)). **[high]** for the facts.
- **Why marketers say it worked** ([Marketing Brew, Aug 1, 2024](https://www.marketingbrew.com/stories/2024/08/01/kamala-hq-is-a-social-marketing-masterclass)) **[med]**:
  - It joined trends early and lightly, without trying to own them.
  - It joined only trends that fit the candidate's real persona.
  - It kept the meme account separate from official messaging.
  - The cautions: memes fade, too much control drains authenticity, and the tone has to turn serious closer to the vote.
- **Insider account** ([Rachel Karten interview with Rob Flaherty, Apr 30, 2025](https://www.linkinbio.news/p/what-did-we-learn-from-kamala-hq)) **[med-high]**:
  - About a dozen mostly under-30 staffers ran it under clear guidelines and light supervision, with a five-minute objection window reserved for existential problems.
  - It worked as an "alt" voice that could do what the candidate couldn't.
  - Authenticity, in Flaherty's view, means content the candidate would plausibly do, not just content that performs.
  - Chasing trends gives a short-term "sugar high", and the account mostly reached people who were already engaged.
- **After the loss** ([Semafor, Dec 15, 2024](https://www.semafor.com/article/12/15/2024/kamala-harris-digital-chief-on-democrats-losing-hold-of-culture)) **[med]**, Flaherty said the campaign:
  - never cracked private networks such as family group chats;
  - should invest in creators and cultural figures and speak the audience's language.

### 2.2 The Mamdani campaign (2025): plain words, a city love letter, candor

- **Slogans that translate.** Greg Sargent describes slogans like "freeze the rent" and "free buses" as concrete and easy to grasp. A campaign adviser said the plain language "translates cleanly into every single language", which matters for immigrant voters ([The New Republic via Portside, Jul 28, 2025](https://portside.org/node/37478)). **[med]**
- **A positive agenda with a whole person in front of it.** Sargent quotes creative director Andrew Epstein on this goal and calls the style "cheerful populism". He notes that short videos on traffic, "halalflation" and endorsements reached millions, many of them non-followers ([same](https://portside.org/node/37478)). **[med]**
- **Message discipline.** Senior adviser Morris Katz describes relentless repetition of a few affordability messages and appearances in everyday places, from games to nightclubs. Materials were adapted to each neighborhood's language and culture ([Center for Digital Action](https://www.centerfordigitalaction.eu/3-digital-progress-magazine/anatomy-of-a-campaign-that-rewrote-the-rules), undated). **[med]**
- **Listening on camera.** In the early Fordham Road video he mostly listened to passersby and revealed he was running only at the end ([The Bulwark, Nov 17, 2025](https://www.thebulwark.com/p/inside-zohran-mamdani-campaign-viral-video-team)). **[med]**
- **The New York love letter.** Jacobin's introduction to its interview with Epstein says the communications "painted a giant love letter" to New York City ([Jacobin, Jan 7, 2026](https://jacobin.com/2026/01/zohran-mamdani-communications-strategy)). **[med]**
- **Candor.** Tubefilter argues his appeal works the way creators' does: plain, unpolished, self-effacing speech that skeptical viewers trust. It also argues that borrowing creator tone without substance reads as performance ([Tubefilter, Jul 28, 2026](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/07/28/zohran-mamdani-creator-discourse-politics-language/)). **[med]**

### 2.3 Gen-Z for Change: creators as messengers, low barriers

- **Self-description.** The organization presents itself as using social media, advocacy and code for progressive change. Its site is plain and earnest, with short noun-phrase headers, Donate and Vote CTAs, and no emoji ([genzforchange.org](https://www.genzforchange.org/)). **[high]** for site copy.
- **Founder's view** (Aidan Kohn-Murphy) ([Thred, Oct 27, 2022](https://thred.com/exclusives/exclusive-how-gen-z-for-change-is-eliminating-barriers-to-action/)) **[med]**:
  - Responsibility for change should feel shared and collective, not individual.
  - Content has to be algorithm-friendly but concise and well researched.
  - Creators' personal stories reach people who aren't searching for politics.

### 2.4 Cringe: what failed and why

- **"Like your abuela" (Dec 2015).** The Clinton campaign's listicle comparing her to Latino grandmothers triggered #NotMyAbuela, and critics called it pandering ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/2016-election/clinton-s-abuela-pitch-latinos-prompts-twitter-backlash-n485166)). **[high]** Lesson: never borrow a community's intimate words to claim kinship you haven't earned. Write Spanish copy in full, reviewed by native speakers, rather than sprinkling in tokens.
- **Student debt "in 3 emojis or less" (Aug 2015).** Reducing a painful issue to emoji struck a nerve, and replies piled on, including grammar corrections ([CNN Money, Aug 12, 2015](https://money.cnn.com/2015/08/12/news/hillary-clinton-tweet-emoji-reaction/)). **[high]** Lesson: emoji never stand in for empathy on a serious subject.
- **"Pokémon Go to the polls" (Jul 2016)** was widely mocked as a forced pun on a trend ([Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/pokemon-go-to-the-polls)). **[med]** Lesson: puns on trending products age in days.
- **House Democrats' shutdown livestream (Oct 1, 2025).** Politico's piece was headlined around a member announcing "Gen Z" guests, under the label of cringe moments. It described a 24-hour plan that ended after about 11.5 hours, reruns overnight, tech glitches, and peak live viewership of about 1,000 ([Politico via Yahoo, Oct 2, 2025](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/shutdown-sort-livestreamed-003452155.html)). **[med-high]** Lessons:
  - Don't name the demographic you're courting.
  - Don't promise a format you can't fill.
- **Brand slang in general.** In Morning Consult's October 2024 survey, 71% of Gen Z had seen brands use internet slang or trends in the past month; 42% liked it and 32% didn't. Gen Z were 14 points more likely than millennials to call it cringey ([Morning Consult, Dec 9, 2024](https://intel.morningconsult.com/mc-content/analysis/brands-social-media-trends)). **[high]**
- **Guilt-and-panic fundraising.** A Nov 2022 survey of Democratic voters found 50% of 18-34-year-olds called Democratic fundraising emails "mostly annoying" and 5% "mostly helpful"; 67% would opt out of all campaign emails and texts if they could ([Election Law Blog, Nov 17, 2022](https://electionlawblog.org/?p=133146); the post names the firm as "Civics", while report 04 calls it Civiqs). **[med]** A Princeton-led study of 2020-cycle political email found clickbait-style manipulation was routine; the median active sender used sensationalist tactics in about 37% of emails ([Mathur et al., Big Data & Society 2023](https://bstewart.scholar.princeton.edu/publications/manipulative-tactics-are-norm-political-emails-evidence-100k-emails-2020-us); [summary](https://deceptive.design/articles/manipulative-tactics-are-the-norm-in-political-emails-evidence-from-100k-emails-from-the-2020-us-election-cycle)). **[high]**
- **The "fossil" baseline.** The national Young Democrats of America homepage leans on all-caps slogans and insider shorthand ("GOTV", "constituency caucuses", "Chapter in a Box") with little explanation ([yda.org](https://www.yda.org/)). **[high]** for the observation. That jargon is exactly what the youngsemdem voice should translate.

## 3. The youngsemdem voice: 12 principles

**The voice in one line:** the friend in your group chat who always knows the deadline, always has a plan for Thursday, and never makes you feel bad for asking.

**Voice dials (0-10):**

| Dial | Setting |
|---|---|
| Warm | 9 |
| Curious | 8 |
| Playful | 6 |
| Urgent | 3, and only when a date is real |
| Formal | 2 |
| Slang | 2: members bring it, the brand doesn't |
| Snark | 1, never at voters, punching only at systems |

1. **Open with a moment, not a mission statement.** Start the way Service95 does: a scene from your week (a Riverwalk cleanup, a clipboard outside a polling place, a taco truck on 17-92), then widen to "we". No "As a progressive organization committed to..." *Basis: 1.2 openers.* **[high]**
2. **One person writing to one person.** First person singular in letters, bylined with a first name and city (demo names, clearly labeled). Use "we" for the team and "you" for the reader. Avoid "the youth", "young people today", "Gen Z" as a label, and "folks". *Basis: 1.3 pronouns; 2.4 shutdown stream.* **[high]**
3. **Warm, not breathless.** Medium sentences with a short punchline. At most one exclamation mark per letter or screen. Zero emoji in letters and long-form copy. One honest superlative per piece (don't copy the "incredible" tic). *Basis: 1.3 counts.* **[high]**
4. **Curate like a friend: three to five picks, each with a reason.** "5 things to do in Seminole this week", each with a concrete why (free, kid-friendly, there's parking, bring a jacket). Free and cheap first, which fixes the Crimson privilege critique. *Basis: 1.4.* **[high]**
5. **Good judgement, no lecture.** Talk like the Ask column: admit when something is hard, give one practical step, skip platitudes. Never tell people how they should feel about an election. *Basis: Ask Dua, Feb 2026.* **[high]**
6. **Curious and unintimidating.** Explain every acronym the first time (or don't use it). Welcome first-timers explicitly. No pop quizzes: you can come to the meeting without having "done the reading". *Basis: Book Club hosting guide; YDA jargon contrast.* **[high]**
7. **Plain words that translate.** Concrete nouns (rent, bus, ballot, Tuesday, 7 pm, Sanford), short slogans, no idioms that break in Spanish. Write so that a full Spanish version reads naturally. *Basis: Mamdani adviser via TNR.* **[med]**
8. **Serious means serious.** When the topic is hard (an election loss, a hurricane, an attack on rights): facts first, then one honest feeling, then one concrete action. 9. **Optimism with receipts.** Hope is a stance, backed by real, checkable wins (local or national) and a next step. Say it plainly when something was lost, and that it isn't final. Never invent a number to sound hopeful. *Basis: Handmaid's essay.* **[high]**

11. **Wink, don't wear the meme.** React fast and lightly to local moments. Never adopt a trend that doesn't fit, never pun on a product, never copy an artist's visual brand (the brat look is someone else's and two years old), and never narrate the audience. Members' videos can be as slangy as they like; brand copy stays plain. *Basis: Kamala HQ, Morning Consult, shutdown stream.* **[high/med]**
12. **Rituals over noise.** Keep the same named sections every week, the same sign-off, a promised cadence (weekly), and no guilt or fake urgency. Consistency is what makes a letter feel like a friend's. *Basis: Service95 series and sign-off; fundraising-email data.* **[high]**

### Words and phrases we use

- **People:** you, we, neighbors, friends, first-timers, members, volunteers, your people, the group chat, regulars, newcomers.
- **Places (local, specific):** Sanford, Lake Mary, Oviedo, Winter Springs, Altamonte Springs, Casselberry, Longwood, Geneva, Chuluota, the Riverwalk, 17-92, the county commission, the school board, the Supervisor of Elections.
- **Actions:** show up, pull up a chair, save your seat, bring a friend, make a plan, check your registration, renew your mail ballot request, host, text one person, ask anything.
- **Warmth:** honestly, real talk, glad you're here, first time? perfect, no experience needed, come as you are, snacks provided, rides available (only when true).
- **Curation:** this week's picks, the list, one thing to do, spotlight, guest letter, the debrief, the recap, receipts (meaning proof of where money went).
- **Hope with substance:** next step, for now, we're building, the long game, small wins count, here's what changed.
- **Inclusive defaults:** everyone, all ages 40 and under (confirm the eligibility wording), LGBTQ+, disabled people and people with disabilities (follow each person's preference), Latino/Latina/Hispanic, pronouns (optional).

### Words and phrases we never use

- **Seminole brand safety:** warrior(s), tribe/tribal (for us), chief, brave(s), war paint, war cry, powwow, spirit animal, on the warpath, circle the wagons, "Noles"-style mascot riffs, any Native imagery or motifs.
- **Fossil and insider talk:** electeds, the base, GOTV, turnout universe, constituency caucus, stakeholders, leverage, synergy, actualize, "the most important election of our lifetime", "fight for the soul of the nation", unprecedented (unless literally true).
- **Panic fundraising:** URGENT, FINAL NOTICE, your membership will lapse, we're begging, 800% match, "you haven't given yet", "we noticed you", fake reply-thread subject lines ("Re: your donation").
- **Fellow-kids slang from the brand voice:** slay, yass, no cap, bestie, rizz, delulu, it's giving, understood the assignment, main character, "Gen Z is in the house". Members can say what they like in their own videos.
- **Belittling voters:** low-information voters, uneducated, sheep, wake up, "do your research".
- **Ableist filler:** crazy, insane, lame, blind to, tone-deaf, "fall on deaf ears". Use wild, intense, crowded, overlooked, ignored.
- **Contested or othering identity terms:** "Latinx" in body copy (Pew found 4% of Hispanic adults use it; see [Pew, Sep 12, 2024](https://www.pewresearch.org/race-and-ethnicity/2024/09/12/latinx-awareness-has-doubled-among-u-s-hispanics-since-2019-but-only-4-percent-use-it/)), illegals, "the gays", "the disabled".

### Use with care

- **"Fight":** fine for issues ("fight for lower rent"), not for people.
- **Profanity:** none in site copy. Her 2025-2026 pieces use it, but a local party-affiliated site has more to lose.
- **Humor about opponents:** aim it at policies and systems, never at voters or at people's looks.
- **"Democracy":** use concretely ("your vote counts in the March election"), not as an abstraction.

## 6. Formatting rules

### Case and caps

- **Headlines and UI:** sentence case ("Save your seat", not "Save Your Seat"). Service95 uses Title Case; sentence case reads more current and is easier to translate.
- **Eyebrow labels and tags:** short ALL CAPS is fine ("THIS WEEK", "GUEST LETTER"), letter-spaced, 1-3 words.
- **Social hype:** one ALL-CAPS word or short phrase per post for emphasis ("WE FILLED THE ROOM"), as she does. Never a whole caption in caps.
- **No all-lowercase branding.** It is someone else's look, and lowercase-only headings hurt readability for some readers.
- **Buttons:** sentence case, verb first, 1-4 words.

### Emoji

- **Letters, essays, serious notices, error messages:** zero.
- **UI success moments and social:** at most one per message. It must decorate, never replace a word, and sit at the end of a line.
- **Social milestones:** use minimalism (a date and one emoji), as she does for big moments.
- **Never use emoji** on grief, violence, disasters or money troubles.
- **Accessibility:** screen readers announce emoji by name, so don't use a run of three. Never use sparkle emoji around an election result.

### Punctuation and mechanics

- **Exclamation marks:** at most one per screen or letter; zero on serious topics; "!!!" only in a hype social caption.
- **Dashes:** an em dash with no spaces (American style) for asides, sparingly. Parentheses carry small jokes and links, as they do in her letters.
- **Ellipsis:** at most once per piece, only to lead into a list ("Here's what's on this week...").
- **Questions:** one self-answered rhetorical question per letter, max ("Small step? Renew your mail ballot request.").
- **Contractions:** always (you're, we'll, don't).
- **Oxford comma:** yes.
- **Spelling:** American English (favorite, organize, neighbors), unlike Service95's British spelling.
- **Numbers:** numerals for 1 and up in UI and lists ("5 picks"); spell out only to start a sentence.
- **Times:** am/pm, lowercase, with a space ("7 pm", "10:30 am"). Dates in the form "Thu, Dec 3" or "Thursday, December 3".
- **Links:** descriptive link text ("Read the full voting guide"), never "click here" (Service95 uses "Click Here"; it fails accessibility).
- **Length:** letters 120-200 words; list blurbs 1-3 sentences; button labels 1-4 words; error messages 2 sentences max (what happened, what to do).
- **Spanish:** full translations, reviewed by a fluent human (Seminole County is about 24% Hispanic or Latino per [Census QuickFacts](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/seminolecountyflorida/PST045224), 2024 estimate, **[med]**, seen via search because the page did not load directly; Puerto Rican residents are often estimated at about 9-10%, **[low]**, older data). Avoid idioms that don't travel.

## 7. Adapting the voice to a local Democratic youth org (without the celebrity)

1. **Swap the glamour for access.** Her "I" travels to Tokyo and Taipei. Ours goes to the Oviedo farmers market and a Lake Mary coffee shop's back room. Keep the sensory specifics, but make them free, nearby and doable on a Tuesday. This fixes the privilege critique in [the Crimson review](https://www.thecrimson.com/article/2022/2/26/dua-lipa-newsletter-podcast-review/).
2. **Make the "I" a rotating member, not a star.** Each letter is bylined by a volunteer (first name and city). In the demo, use clearly fictional names marked "(demo)". The organization stays "we". Guest letters hand over the mic, as with the Malala guest edit.
3. **Keep her structure, and rename everything.**

   | Service95 element | youngsemdem version |
   |---|---|
   | Weekly letter | "The Thursday Letter" |
   | Dua's List | "The Picks" |
   | 10 Questions | "Talk About It" (meeting or watch-party prompts) |
   | The Way I Work... | "How I Got Involved" |
   | Ask Dua | "Ask the Group Chat" |
   | Guest Edit | "Guest Letter" |

   None of these echo her brand names.
4. **Keep her serious-topic pattern exactly:** facts, a short personal stake, one feeling, one action. This is the most transferable part of her voice and the part political orgs most often get wrong.
5. **Pick our own sign-off and keep it forever.** Options:
   - (a) "See you Thursday. [Name], for the Young Sem Dems xo"
   - (b) "Text a friend. [Name] xo"
   - (c) "Here's to the long game. [Name]"

   I recommend (a): it carries a ritual, a cadence and a kiss without copying her "Dua x" line.
6. **Never trade on her.** No name, photos, song titles, lyric puns, "Service" puns, or "inspired by Dua Lipa" copy on the site. The voice is a craft reference, not a brand association.
7. **Write for two registers, as she does.** Long-form (letters, explainers) is calm, warm and emoji-free. Short-form (buttons, toasts, social) can be peppier, with one emoji and one caps word.
8. **Separate the tones by section.** The site should never put "The Picks" and a post-election debrief in the same card or the same email block. Use sections and color to signal the shift, which avoids the "jarring" mix the Crimson reviewer flagged.
9. **Write the copy so it translates first.** Short slogans in the Mamdani mould ("Rent is too high. Let's fix it locally.") read cleanly in Spanish. Puns and memes don't.
10. **Use Claude visibly, and in this voice.** The "Ask" panel and any "draft my invite" tool should use a system prompt built from sections 3-6, label itself as AI, link official sources, and refuse to invent numbers or quotes. (Report 04 covers why disclosure matters to this audience.)

## 10. Facts behind the sample lines

- **Mail ballot requests.** A Florida request covers all elections from the request date through the end of the calendar year of the next regularly scheduled general election, and must be renewed after that. Requests made before the Nov 3, 2026 general therefore lapse at the end of 2026. A request made now should cover through the end of 2028 ([Florida Division of Elections, vote-by-mail](https://dos.fl.gov/elections/for-voters/voting/vote-by-mail/)). **[high]** for the rule; **[med]** for the 2028 reading, so confirm with the Supervisor of Elections.
- **Registration and primaries.** Registration closes 29 days before an election, and party changes are due 29 days before a primary. Floridians can pre-register at 16 ([Florida Division of Elections, register](https://dos.fl.gov/elections/for-voters/voter-registration/register-to-vote-or-update-your-information/)). **[high]**
- **Mail ballot mailing deadline.** Requests to have a ballot mailed are due by 5 pm on the 12th day before an election ([same vote-by-mail page](https://dos.fl.gov/elections/for-voters/voting/vote-by-mail/)). **[high]**
