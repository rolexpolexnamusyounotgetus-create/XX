## Headline

Seminole is a young-ish, highly educated, roughly one-quarter Hispanic suburban county. It is also, right now, the best news story in Central Florida for Democrats. Unofficial November 3, 2026 returns show Democrats carrying the county in governor (Jolly 54.0%), U.S. Senate, every Cabinet race, the state Senate seat and three of four state House contests, plus a countywide County Commission seat. That is a sharp turn from 2024, when Trump won Seminole 50.9% to 47.4%. Statewide, though, Republicans held the governorship by about 1.4 points. **Voters aged 18 to 40 are 36.8% of Seminole's 338,692 active voters (124,626 people). In that group, no-party-affiliation (NPA) voters are the largest bloc (34.3%), ahead of Democrats (32.7%) and Republicans (27.4%).** That one fact should shape the site's tone: it is talking mostly to persuadable independents, not party loyalists.

Three findings change what the site can say:

1. **A chapter already exists.** "Young Democrats of Seminole County" (YDSC) is listed by Florida Young Democrats with the Instagram handle **@youngsemdems**, one letter away from this project's working handle. Florida Young Democrats allows only one chapter per county.
2. **The age line conflicts with the bylaws.** Both FYD's charter and YDSC's own dues page cap membership at "less than 40". The national Young Democrats of America charter only guarantees membership up to age 35. So "40 and under" contradicts the actual bylaws.
3. **The voting rules change on January 1, 2027.** Florida's new elections law, HB 991, removes **student IDs** (and debit and credit cards) from the list of acceptable voter ID and adds citizenship-verification steps for new registrants. Separately, every mail-ballot request expires on December 31, 2026, so voters must re-request for the 2027 city elections and the 2028 cycle.

The site's most useful service will be getting this right, in English and Spanish.

**Chartering requirements if anyone ever needed to charter or recharter** ([FYD Bylaws Art. II §2.1.2-2.1.3](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1VW0gD0UWFJuHokobhdMLnzjMBYV9UWIl/view)) [high]:

- a member list of at least 5 (name, address, phone, email, date of birth);
- the elected officers and the date they were elected;
- the chapter's governing documents;
- minutes from a meeting within the past six months, plus a copy of the meeting notice;
- $50 annual dues to FYD.

Paperwork is due by January 31, and charters are granted by April 15. A new chapter can apply only if the county has had no active charter in the past 90 days, which shuts out a second Seminole chapter while YDSC holds one.

**Bottom line:** say **"under 40"** (members 14 to 39; voters 18 to 39), or simply "Gen Z + millennials", unless YDSC's own bylaws say otherwise. "40 and under" is off by one year against both FYD and YDSC [high].

### 3b. The rules

- **Eligibility and pre-registration at 16:** to register you must be a U.S. citizen, a Florida resident and a resident of the county, and at least 18. You can **pre-register from your 16th birthday**, and you can vote in any election held on or after your 18th birthday ([F.S. 97.041](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/97.041)) [high]. The SOE says a pre-registration turns active on the 18th birthday and a voter card is mailed then ([SOE](https://voteseminole.gov/voters/register-to-vote/)) [high].
- **Registration deadline:** the books close on the **29th day before each election**. If that day falls on a Sunday or a legal holiday, they close the next day. Applications received after book closing count only for later elections. There is no same-day registration ([F.S. 97.055](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/97.055)) [high]. Name, address and signature updates are still allowed after book closing. Party changes after book closing do not apply to that primary (same statute).
- **Closed primaries:** a voter can vote only the primary ballot of the party they are registered with ([F.S. 101.021](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/101.021)) [high]. **The exception:** if every candidate for an office is in the same party and the winner will face no opposition in November, all voters may vote in that primary ([Fla. Const. Art. VI §5(b)](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Constitution)) [high]. Nonpartisan races (school board, judges, city races) are open to everyone. *This matters because NPA voters are the largest bloc among Seminole voters under 40 (§6c).*
- **Vote-by-mail requests: how long they last.** One request covers all elections **through the end of the calendar year of the next regularly scheduled general election**, unless the voter names specific elections ([F.S. 101.62(1)(a)](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/101.62)) [high].
  - In practice, **every request on file expires on December 31, 2026**, and voters must request again for the 2027 municipal elections and for 2028 [high on the rule; med on how late-2026 requests are treated].
  - Precedent: Oviedo's elections page says all requests expired on 12/31/2024 ([City of Oviedo](https://cityofoviedo.net/724/Elections)). Osceola's elections office said everything on file expired on January 1, 2025, including requests made after the November 2024 election ([Around Osceola, Jan 29, 2026](https://www.aroundosceola.com/news/voting-mail-26-make-sure-your-request-valid)) [med: a neighboring county's reading, but consistent with the statute].
  - **Site copy:** "Renew your mail ballot request in January 2027." Confirm the exact start date with the Seminole SOE.
- **Requesting a mail ballot:** in person or by phone you need a Florida driver license number, a Florida ID number, or the last four digits of your Social Security number. A written request must be signed and include one of those numbers. A family member or guardian can request only if the voter directly asks them to ([F.S. 101.62(1)(b)](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/101.62)) [high]. Requests to have a ballot mailed are due by **5:00 pm on the 12th day before** the election ([F.S. 101.62(3)](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/101.62)) [high].
- **Returning a mail ballot:** it must be **received by 7:00 pm on Election Day**; postmarks don't count. You can mail it, deliver it to the SOE office during business hours, or use a secure intake station at early-voting sites during early-voting hours. The signature-cure deadline is 5:00 pm on the second day after the election ([SOE vote-by-mail](https://voteseminole.gov/how-to-vote/vote-by-mail/)) [high].
- **Early voting:** by law it runs from the **10th to the 3rd day before** an election with state or federal races, 8 to 12 hours a day. Supervisors may add the 15th to 11th days and the 2nd day ([F.S. 101.657(1)(d)](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/101.657)) [high]. For the 2026 general, Seminole offered the maximum: Oct 19 to Nov 1, 7:00 am to 7:00 pm. For the 2026 primary it offered only the minimum: Aug 8 to 15, 10:00 am to 6:00 pm ([SOE 2026 Election Facts](https://voteseminole.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/2026-Election-Facts.pdf)) [high].
- **Voter ID through December 31, 2026:** a current photo ID from the statutory list. The list includes a Florida driver license, Florida ID card, U.S. passport, **debit or credit card**, military ID, **student ID**, retirement-center ID, neighborhood-association ID, public-assistance ID, VA health card, concealed-carry license, and a government employee ID. If the photo ID has no signature, a second ID with a signature is needed. Without ID, the voter casts a provisional ballot ([F.S. 101.043](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/101.043)) [high].
- **Voter ID from January 1, 2027 (HB 991, Ch. 2026-26, signed April 1, 2026)** ([final bill analysis](https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2026/991/Analyses/h0991z1.GOS.PDF); [bill page](https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2026/991)) [high on the enacted text; see Open questions on litigation]:
  - **Removed:** debit and credit cards, **student IDs**, retirement-center, neighborhood-association and public-assistance IDs.
  - **Added:** the U.S. passport card, and any ID issued by a federal, state, county or municipal government entity. **IDs issued by an educational institution are excluded even if the school is public.** A UCF or Seminole State card will not work [high on the text; med on how SOEs will apply it to every card type].
  - The same ID list applies to first-time voters voting by mail and to provisional and mail-ballot cure affidavits.
  - The Seminole SOE says nothing in HB 991 changes the 2026 elections, and that provisions affecting voters start January 1, 2027 ([SOE newsletter, May 2026](https://voteseminole.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Ballots-And-Beyond-Edition-7.pdf)) [high].
- **Citizenship checks for new registrants from January 1, 2027 (HB 991):**
  - If state driver-license records show an applicant is not a citizen or has not provided proof of citizenship, or the applicant has no Florida DL, ID or SSN, the supervisor must verify citizenship.
  - If citizenship can't be verified by book closing, the voter must show proof before voting or gets a **provisional ballot**. That ballot counts only if citizenship is verified by 5:00 pm on the second day after the election.
  - Online applicants without a Florida DL or ID get a pre-filled form to print, sign and deliver.
  - Acceptable proof includes a U.S. birth certificate, U.S. passport, naturalization certificate, consular Report of Birth Abroad, or a Florida DL or ID that shows citizenship. By July 1, 2027, new and renewed Florida licenses must show legal status. ([HB 991 final analysis](https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2026/991/Analyses/h0991z1.GOS.PDF)) [high]
- **Petition circulating is now legally risky for volunteers:**
  - Under HB 1205 (2025, law since May 2, 2025), anyone collecting more than 25 signed petition forms beyond their own and immediate family's must register as a circulator ([HB 1205](https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2025/1205); described in [Central Florida Public Media, Feb 2, 2026](https://www.cfpublic.org/2026-02-02/florida-says-2026-ballot-citizen-led-amendments-failed-including-recreational-marijuana)) [high].
  - HB 991 adds some petition violations to Florida's RICO racketeering law, effective July 1, 2026 ([HB 991 analysis](https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2026/991/Analyses/h0991z1.GOS.PDF)) [high].
  - The site must not casually urge members to "collect signatures".

## 7. Local texture for copy

- **The cities:** seven incorporated cities (Altamonte Springs, Casselberry, Lake Mary, Longwood, Oviedo, Sanford, Winter Springs), plus large unincorporated areas where half the voters live ([SOE](https://voteseminole.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Totals-of-Voters-by-Cities.pdf)) [high]. The county was carved from Orange County in 1913 and is named for the Seminole people ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seminole_County,_Florida)) [med]. **That name is exactly why the site must avoid any Native imagery** (see §9).
- **Sanford** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanford,_Florida)) [med]:
  - the county seat, on the south shore of **Lake Monroe** on the St. Johns River;
  - a lakefront **Riverwalk** first completed in 2004 and since extended, and a historic downtown with a marina;
  - nicknamed the **"Celery City"** after celery farming began there in 1896;
  - home to Orlando Sanford International Airport and the southern end of Amtrak's Auto Train;
  - civil-rights history: Jackie Robinson was driven off the field during 1946 spring training, and the 2012 killing of Trayvon Martin happened here. Handle with care, never as a gimmick.
- **Longwood's Big Tree Park** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Senator_(tree))) [med]:
  - home of **The Senator**, a bald cypress about 3,500 years old that burned in January 2012;
  - **Lady Liberty**, about 2,000 years old, still stands nearby;
  - a clone called **"The Phoenix"** was planted when the park reopened in 2014. That is a ready-made "rise from the ashes" metaphor.
- **Oviedo's chickens:** the downtown free-roaming chickens are local legend. Wikipedia says growth has largely ended the roaming, so treat them as nostalgia and folklore, not a present-day fact ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oviedo,_Florida)) [med]. Oviedo grew partly because of UCF next door (same source).
- **Lake Jesup** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Jesup)) [med]:
  - the county's largest lake, about 16,000 acres including floodplain;
  - one of Florida's densest alligator populations;
  - badly degraded by the 1960s from wastewater, stormwater and berms, with restoration since the 1990s (berm removal in 1996; SR 46 bridge and causeway work in 2008).
- **Wekiva River** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wekiva_River)) [med]:
  - a 16-mile river along Seminole's western edge (the Orange and Lake borders);
  - a National **Wild and Scenic River** since October 13, 2000;
  - **Wekiwa Springs State Park itself is in Apopka, Orange County, not in Seminole.** Say "the Wekiva" rather than implying the springs are in the county.
- **SunRail:** four of its 17 stations are in Seminole: **Altamonte Springs, Longwood, Lake Mary, Sanford** ([SunRail](https://sunrail.com/stations/)) [high].
- **Colleges:**
  - **Seminole State College:** campuses in Altamonte Springs, Heathrow, Oviedo and Sanford/Lake Mary; offers bachelor's degrees and a Direct Connect pathway to UCF ([Seminole State](https://www.seminolestate.edu/about)) [high]. Its Oviedo campus Building F100 served as a 2024 polling place ([SOE 2024 results](https://voteseminole.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/2024GeneralElection-Official-PrecinctSummary-Groups-Redacted-Public.pdf)) [high].
  - **UCF:** 70,674 students, main campus in east Orlando, bordering Oviedo ([UCF](https://www.ucf.edu/about-ucf/)) [high on figure; the page doesn't give the term].
- **Civic detail:** Seminole's 2026 "I Voted" sticker was its first student-designed sticker, by a sixth grader at Tuskawilla Middle ([SOE newsletter, May 2026](https://voteseminole.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Ballots-And-Beyond-Edition-7.pdf)) [high]. That is a nice precedent for a youngsemdem sticker or design contest.

## 9. Recommendations for the youngsemdem site

1. **Settle the name before anything is public.** Recommended framing: *"a demo site built for the Young Democrats of Seminole County"*, shown to YDSC (YoungDems@SemDems.com, @youngsemdems) and SemDems for sign-off.
   - "youngsemdem" sits one letter from the existing chapter's handle, and FYD allows one chapter per county ([FYD Bylaws](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1VW0gD0UWFJuHokobhdMLnzjMBYV9UWIl/view)).
   - If Kira is not working with YDSC, choose a clearly distinct name, so the site cannot be mistaken for the chartered chapter or as speaking for SemDems or FYD.
   - Put a visible "Demo / not an official site of any party organization" banner on the demo.
   - Since youngsemdems.com shows a suspended hosting page, ask YDSC who controls it before buying any lookalike domain.
2. **Use "under 40", not "40 and under".** Say "Gen Z + millennials under 40" in copy and "ages 14 to 39" on membership forms (voting age 18), matching FYD and YDSC. The data stories can still use the SOE's "18 to 40" voter band, labeled as such.
3. **Write for independents, not insiders.** NPA is the largest group of Seminole voters under 40 (34.3%). Lead with issues and community, not party jargon. Add a 30-second explainer: "NPA? You can't vote in Florida's party primaries, but you can vote in city, school board and judge races, and in November."
4. **Build a "2027 Ready" voting hub**, bilingual (EN/ES), with five cards linked only to official sources:
   - (a) **Renew your mail ballot**: all requests expire December 31, 2026;
   - (b) **Your student ID won't work at the polls from January 1, 2027**: bring a Florida DL or ID, a passport or passport card, or another government ID;
   - (c) check registration and register (pre-register at 16);
   - (d) find your polling place and track your ballot;
   - (e) key dates: Nov 2, 2027 city elections (Oviedo confirmed); Mar 21, 2028 PPP; Aug 22, 2028 primary; Nov 7, 2028 general.

   Mark each computed deadline "confirm with the Supervisor of Elections", and stamp "last verified" dates.
5. **Keep every date and link in one data file** (for example `data/civic.json`: elections, deadlines, official URLs, last-verified date, source URL). Pages render from it. When the site moves to a real domain, one file gets updated rather than many pages edited, and an "expires" field can auto-hide stale deadlines.
6. **Tell the November 3 story honestly.** Use "Seminole voted blue" hero stats (Jolly 54% in Seminole; Democrats led three of four state House contests in the county; a countywide commission seat; 57% turnout), always labeled **"unofficial: certification due Nov 16, 2026"**, and update after certification. Pair it with the statewide reality (Republicans held governor and Senate) to motivate rather than gloat. **Don't put candidates' names or faces on the site as if they endorse the group**, per the brand rules.
7. **Lead with data that is both shareable and sourced:**
   - "1 in 3 Seminole voters is 40 or under (124,626 of us)";
   - "Seminole cleared 60% for Amendment 4 in 2024";
   - "Half of Seminole voters live outside any city";
   - "Only 0.5% of us commute by transit".

   Link each to its source, so screenshots carry credibility.
8. **Make Spanish a launch feature, not an add-on.** Hispanic residents are 25.4% of the county, 23.1% speak a language other than English at home, the county ballot itself is bilingual, and the county party credits Spanish-language outreach. Have the Spanish reviewed by a native speaker, ideally a Puerto Rican and broader Latin American reader panel.
9. **Brand safety around the name "Seminole".**
   - The [Seminole Tribe of Florida](https://www.semtribe.com/) is a sovereign nation. Use no Native imagery, patterns, feathers, "tribe" or "warrior" language, and no FSU-style references.
   - Build the visual identity from place instead: Celery City lime, Lake Monroe sunsets, SunRail, cypress (The Senator and the Phoenix), Lake Jesup gators, Oviedo chickens as playful nostalgia, the Riverwalk.
10. **Protect volunteers from the new petition and ID laws.** No "go collect signatures" calls to action without circulator-registration guidance ([HB 1205](https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2025/1205); [HB 991](https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2026/991/Analyses/h0991z1.GOS.PDF)). A "Know the 2027 rules" explainer is itself shareable content.
11. **Plug into what already exists.** Add a "Show up" module pointing to public SemDems events (monthly meeting, second Thursday, 7:00 pm; the Disability and Progressive caucuses), with YDSC's own meetups once confirmed. Feature Seminole State and UCF students explicitly, especially because of the student-ID change.
12. **Issue pages should cite or stay quiet.** Rent, insurance and heat pages need sourced local numbers before launch (see Open questions). Until then, use first-person "what we're hearing" framing, clearly marked demo, never invented statistics.
