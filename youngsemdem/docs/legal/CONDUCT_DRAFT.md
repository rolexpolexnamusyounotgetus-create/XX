The Young Democrats of Seminole County (YDSC) is a club for people **14 to 39** (YDSC dues page on
ActBlue; `org.minAge: 14` in `assets/js/config.js`). Lots of people here are new to politics, some
are teenagers, and everyone should be able to show up without being harassed, recorded, doxxed or
pressured. This code says how members and guests treat each other at events and online, what's not
OK, how to report a problem and what happens next.

Two other policies add rules on top of this one, and where they are stricter, they win:
the [Teen Safety Policy](MINORS_POLICY.md) (photos of minors, DMs, events teens attend) and the
[Clip Rules](MODERATION_POLICY.md) (what can be posted to the clip feed).

## The short version
<!-- synced word for word with data/legal.js pages.conduct.tldr (EN), Nov 9, 2026. Numbers the sections
     below mark *(proposal)* are still proposals for Kira and the board. Edit both together. -->

- Be kind. Argue about ideas, never attack people.
- No harassment, threats or slurs, and no touching, flirting or photos nobody asked for.
- No doxxing and no lists of private people. Ever.
- Nobody records or posts anyone under 18 without a parent's or guardian's OK.
- Something wrong? Tell the event's safety lead or email `{privacyEmail}`. We follow up within 24 hours.
- Breaking these rules can mean a warning, being asked to leave, or losing your membership.

## 1. Who does this cover?
<!-- page: conduct.html#conduct-who -->

**People.** Everyone in a YDSC space: members (voting and non-voting), guests, volunteers,
organizers and officers. Supporters on the email list (people 40 and up can join the list as
supporters, not members) are covered as guests.

**Spaces.**

- Events, meetings, trainings and calls YDSC runs or co-hosts, in person or online.
- Every online space YDSC runs: comments, the clip feed, texts, email lists and any group chat an
  organizer sets up for the club.
- Volunteer shifts: text banks, neighbor check-ins (door-knocking), tables.
- How you treat people while you're there **with us** somewhere else, like a rally, a partner's
  event or a door you're knocking on.

**Not covered.** Your personal social media when you're not acting for YDSC, except where it
targets someone in a YDSC space (harassing a member after a meeting counts).

## 2. What do you expect from me?
<!-- page: conduct.html#conduct-expect -->

- **Assume good faith.** Lots of people here are new to politics, and asking questions is the point.
  "What does that mean?" is always a fair question.
- **Disagree about ideas, not people.** Criticize policies and politicians all you want, Democrats
  included; don't go after the person in front of you. The biggest group of young Seminole voters
  isn't registered with any party: invite people in, don't scold them.
- **Use the names and pronouns people give you.** Every accent and language is welcome, Spanish
  included. Don't correct someone's English or ask where they're "really" from.
- **Respect a no:** in conversations, photos, hugs and everything else. Ask before you take or post
  a photo of someone, and take it down if they ask.
- **Look out for each other.** If someone seems uncomfortable, check in or get an organizer.
- **No alcohol for anyone under 21.** Events teens attend are alcohol-free or keep alcohol in a
  separate, ID-checked area (Fla. Stat. 562.11; Teen Safety Policy, section 5). YDSC's own demo
  events are all alcohol-free.

## 3. What's not OK?
<!-- page: conduct.html#conduct-not -->

1. **Harassment:** insults, slurs, threats, intimidation, stalking, or following someone around
   (in person or online) after they've asked you to stop. Repeated unwanted messages count.
2. **Unwanted touching, sexual comments, or advances nobody asked for.** Any romantic or sexual
   contact between an adult and a minor, ever, including messages.
3. **Hate:** attacks on people for race, ethnicity, national origin, immigration status, religion,
   sex, gender identity, sexual orientation, disability or age.
4. **Doxxing:** sharing anyone's home address, phone number, school, workplace, license plate or
   voter-record details without their OK. Even when the information is public somewhere, using it
   to find, shame or target a person is not OK here.
5. **Lists of private people:** no watch lists, spreadsheets or posts that target private
   individuals, including people who disagree with us. Public officials' public actions are fair
   to criticize; their families and home addresses are not.
6. **Recording or posting people who didn't agree to it.** Florida law generally requires everyone's
   consent to record a private conversation (Fla. Stat. 934.03, with "oral communication" defined in
   934.02). Using someone's name or likeness to promote something needs their consent, and a
   parent's or guardian's for a minor (Fla. Stat. 540.08). **Never record or post anyone under 18
   without a parent's or guardian's OK** (Teen Safety Policy, section 3).
7. **Pressuring voters, election workers or people who disagree with us.** We never solicit voters
   inside a voting site or within 150 feet of its entrance or a ballot drop box: no handouts, no
   asking how someone will vote, no giving out items (Fla. Stat. 102.031(4)). Intimidating or
   misleading voters is a crime (Fla. Stat. 104.0615). On voting rules, point people to the
   Seminole County Supervisor of Elections instead of guessing.
8. **Retaliation:** punishing, outing or pressuring someone because they reported a problem or
   helped with a report.
9. **Lying about who you are** in a YDSC space, including posing as an officer, an election
   official or another member.

## 4. How do I report something?
<!-- page: conduct.html#conduct-report -->

- **Someone in danger right now: call 911.**
- **At an event:** tell the safety lead (we introduce them at check-in) or any organizer.
- **Online or afterward:** email `{privacyEmail}`. You don't have to give your name.
  `[BUILD: there is no report form; email only. legal.privacyEmail is '' in config.js, so pages show
  a placeholder until the chapter sets an address. Recommendation: a dedicated safety address.]`
- **On a clip:** tap Report (Clip Rules, section 4).
- **Suspected child abuse:** call the Florida Abuse Hotline, **1-800-962-2873**, any time. In
  Florida, anyone who suspects it must report it (Fla. Stat. 39.201; Florida DCF). Reporting to us
  does not replace that call.

Tell us what happened, when and where, who was involved and anyone who saw it, as far as you're
comfortable. Screenshots help; never forward intimate images, even as evidence: describe them and
we'll ask the right people to handle them.

## 5. Who replies, and how fast?
<!-- page: conduct.html#conduct-reply -->

- **Who:** organizers on the club's safety team read every report. This is a demo, so that team
  isn't named yet: `[OPEN: the chapter picks at least two people, ideally not both officers, before
  launch, plus the youth safety officer for anything involving a minor (Teen Safety Policy, section 8)]`.
- **How fast:** we follow up within **24 hours** *(proposal)*. Anything involving a minor or a
  threat is handled the same day.
- **Conflicts:** nobody involved in a report, or close to someone who is, helps decide it.
- **Privacy:** reports stay private. We share them only with the people who need to act, or when
  the law requires it. We keep the record short and store it where only the safety team can see it
  `[OPEN: where, and for how long; see Privacy Policy, section 12]`.
- **No payback:** nobody gets punished for reporting in good faith.

## 6. What happens next?
<!-- page: conduct.html#conduct-next -->

**Responses** depend on what happened and on whether it has happened before:

1. a private talk;
2. a written warning;
3. being asked to leave an event (the safety lead can do this on the spot);
4. a break from events or online spaces for a set time;
5. losing membership.

**Serious harm,** like threats, violence, sexual misconduct, doxxing or anything involving a minor,
can mean removal right away and, when the law requires it, a report to the authorities.

**Appeals.** Think we got it wrong? Email `{privacyEmail}` within **14 days**. Two organizers who
weren't part of the first decision review it and answer within **7 days** *(proposal)*.

`[OPEN for counsel and the board: check YDSC's governing documents and the Florida Young Democrats
charter and bylaws for any procedure on suspending or removing a member (FYD's rules say a chapter's

it can still ask someone to leave an event or a chat right away.]`

## 7. Event safety basics
<!-- not on the public page (conduct.html) -->

*(proposal)* Every event names a **safety lead** who is introduced at check-in, knows the venue's
exits, carries the Florida Abuse Hotline number, and has the authority to ask someone to leave.
Events teens attend follow the Teen Safety Policy, section 5 (two adults, guardian permission for
off-site or after-dark events). Event pages carry `snippets.safetyLine` from `data/legal.js`.

## 8. Training and records
<!-- not on the public page (conduct.html) -->

*(proposal)* Before leading an event or running a chat, organizers read this code and the Teen
Safety Policy and confirm in writing. The safety team keeps a short log of reports (date, what,
outcome), never in a shared group chat, and reviews this code once a year or after any serious
incident.
