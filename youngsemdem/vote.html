Why does it end?

In Florida, one mail-ballot request lasts from the day you make it through the end of the year of the next regular general election. That election was Nov 3, 2026, so a request made between the Nov 2024 and Nov 2026 elections ends Dec 31, 2026. That’s the law, not a glitch, and it happens every two years.

Renewing before Jan 1? Under the state’s rule, a request made after Nov 3 should already count through 2028. Seminole hasn’t posted how it’s handling these yet, so look yourself up on My Voter Record in January and make sure your request shows.

Sources: F.S. 101.62(1)(a) · Florida Division of Elections

NEW IN 2027

Two rules change on Jan 1, 2027

Student IDs stop counting at the polls

Starting Jan 1, 2027, Florida stops accepting student IDs at the polls (it’s being challenged in court). Bring a driver’s license, state ID, or passport, and check voteseminole.gov before you go.

That means any card a school issues, even a public college or university, like a UCF or Seminole State ID. Debit and credit cards come off the list too.

At check-in you need photo and signature ID. One card with both works. No ID? You can still vote a provisional ballot.

On Oct 16, 2026, the Florida NAACP’s branches and youth units sued in federal court to block the student-ID ban. As of Nov 9, 2026 there’s no ruling, so plan as if the change happens. We’ll update this if a court steps in.

On Nov 9, 2026, the county’s ID page still showed the list used through 2026. Check it again before you vote.

Registrations and updates get a citizenship check

From Jan 1, 2027, Florida checks citizenship when you register or update your record. It starts with driver-license records; if those can’t confirm it, the elections office checks other records and may ask you for proof.

Already registered? Keep your address current and open mail from the elections office. If you get a notice, answer it by the date on it, and call 407-585-8683 with any questions.

Registering for the first time? Do it early, so there’s time to answer if the elections office asks for anything. Several federal lawsuits challenge these rules; as of Nov 9, 2026, we haven’t found any court order pausing them.

    No party? Here’s what you can vote in.

    You don’t have to pick a party to register. No party affiliation (NPA) is a real choice, and either way you’re welcome here.
