REAL Heads up

Mail ballot? Renew it.

Asked for a mail ballot before Nov 3? That request ends Dec 31. Ask again and you should be set through 2028. Check My Voter Record to be sure.

Requests made before Nov 3, 2026 ended Dec 31, 2026. One new request covers every election through Dec 31, 2028.

Renew online

The reminder pops up Mon, Jan 4, 2027 at 10 am, when the office phones are open. Or add it to Google Calendar · Outlook

Not sure when 2027 requests open? The Supervisor of Elections can tell you: 407-585-8683

Have one number ready: your Florida driver license number, your Florida ID card number, or the last four digits of your Social Security number.

Seminole’s vote-by-mail page

Why does it end?

In Florida, one mail-ballot request lasts from the day you make it through the end of the year of the next regular general election. That election was Nov 3, 2026, so a request made between the Nov 2024 and Nov 2026 elections ends Dec 31, 2026. That’s the law, not a glitch, and it happens every two years.

Renewing before Jan 1? Under the state’s rule, a request made after Nov 3 should already count through 2028. Seminole hasn’t posted how it’s handling these yet, so look yourself up on My Voter Record in January and make sure your request shows.

Sources: F.S. 101.62(1)(a) · Florida Division of Elections

NO PARTY?

No party? Here’s what you can vote in.

You don’t have to pick a party to register. No party affiliation (NPA) is a real choice, and either way you’re welcome here.

You vote in

  • Every race in every general election
  • Nonpartisan races, like school board, judges and city races
  • Ballot questions
  • A primary race where every candidate is from one party and the winner faces no one in November

You sit out

  • Party primaries, including the presidential primary on Mar 21, 2028

Want in? You can change party any time. For a primary, do it before registration closes, 29 days ahead.

NEW IN 2027

Two rules change on Jan 1, 2027

Student IDs stop counting at the polls

Starting Jan 1, 2027, Florida stops accepting student IDs at the polls (it’s being challenged in court). Bring a driver’s license, state ID, or passport, and check voteseminole.gov before you go.

Check voteseminole.gov What ID to bring (official)
The details and sources

That means any card a school issues, even a public college or university, like a UCF or Seminole State ID. Debit and credit cards come off the list too.

At check-in you need photo and signature ID. One card with both works. No ID? You can still vote a provisional ballot.

On Oct 16, 2026, the Florida NAACP’s branches and youth units sued in federal court to block the student-ID ban. As of Nov 9, 2026 there’s no ruling, so plan as if the change happens. We’ll update this if a court steps in.

On Nov 9, 2026, the county’s ID page still showed the list used through 2026. Check it again before you vote.

Sources: HB 991 summary · F.S. 101.043 · Lawsuit filed Oct 16, 2026 · Links checked Nov 9, 2026

Registrations and updates get a citizenship check

From Jan 1, 2027, Florida checks citizenship when you register or update your record. It starts with driver-license records; if those can’t confirm it, the elections office checks other records and may ask you for proof.

Register or update online
The details and sources

Already registered? Keep your address current and open mail from the elections office. If you get a notice, answer it by the date on it, and call 407-585-8683 with any questions.

Registering for the first time? Do it early, so there’s time to answer if the elections office asks for anything. Several federal lawsuits challenge these rules; as of Nov 9, 2026, we haven’t found any court order pausing them.

Sources: HB 991 summary · Links checked Nov 9, 2026

    UNDER 18?

    14 to 17? Here’s your version.

    You can’t vote yet, but you’re early, not left out. Here’s what Florida lets you do now, and how to help with us safely.

    1. AT 16

      Pre-register

      Florida lets you pre-register on or after your 16th birthday. It turns into a full registration on its own when you turn 18.

      Pre-register online

    2. AT 18

      You vote

      You can vote in any election on or after your 18th birthday. Turning 18 after the registration deadline but by Election Day? You can vote if you pre-registered before the deadline.

    3. AT 16 OR 17

      Work the polls

      Florida law lets pre-registered voters work the polls, and Seminole’s election-worker rules include them. It’s paid, with a training class and a long Election Day (14 hours or more). Ask the elections office about the 2027 city elections.

      Election worker info (official)

    4. ANY AGE

      Bring it to your school

      The elections office will present to a class or set up a registration table at a high school, for students 16 and up. Ask a teacher to book it.

      School programs (official)

    5. WITH US

      Help with us, safely

      • Join free at 14. Your invite link gets a random code, never your name.
      • Daytime events are yours. Off-site, after dark or a rally? A parent or guardian signs off first.
      • No club texts before 18, and never a one-on-one message from an adult organizer.
      • Help one person who can vote make their plan.
    Join free Make a plan together

    For parents: our teen safety rules

    No prizes, points or stickers for pre-registering or voting, ever. Those steps are yours.
