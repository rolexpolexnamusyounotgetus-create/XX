    How the model works

    ShowHide

    Each simulated fight runs five rounds of five minutes. In every round, each man lands significant strikes at a rate built from his own offense averaged with the other man's defense. Every landed strike carries a small chance of a knockdown, set by career knockdown rates and scaled by the target's chin and the damage he has already taken. Some knockdowns end the fight; the rest swing the round.

    Takedowns buy control time and ground strikes. Damage slows a fighter's output, leg kicks wear down his movement, and Volkanovski's pace rises in the late rounds while Gaethje's dips. Rounds that finish go to three judges who score with shared and individual noise, 10–8 when one man scores two more knockdowns. A corner can stop a fight between rounds once the damage piles up.

    Judgment calls

    At 155 the baseline trims Volkanovski's output to 0.95 of his career rate and his power to 0.85, and makes him 1.15 times easier to drop than an average lightweight. Judges credit a Gaethje strike at 1.10 of a Volkanovski strike. About a quarter of knockdowns end the fight early on, rising past half as damage builds. A Volkanovski takedown is worth about 45 seconds of control, a Gaethje takedown about 40. Gaethje's late fade is an assumption drawn from his last loss, where Max Holloway out-landed him 62 to 18 in the fifth round.
